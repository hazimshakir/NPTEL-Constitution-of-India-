# Features of the Indian Constitution

## Longest Written Constitution

- 470 Articles and 12 Schedules.
- Merges features from the United States, the United Kingdom, Ireland, and Canada.

## Blend of Rigidity and Flexibility

- The Goods and Services Tax (GST) in 2017 shifted taxation powers between the Centre and the States and required the full “rigid” amendment process.
- Changing the name of a state requires only a simple majority, demonstrating flexibility.

## Federal System with a Unitary Bias

### Federal System

- Powers are divided between the Centre and the States.
- States handle police, health, and local matters.
- The Centre handles defence, foreign policy, and national finance.

### Unitary Bias

- The Centre can step in under special circumstances.

## Parliamentary Form of Government

- The President is the formal head of state.
- The Prime Minister and Council of Ministers run the government as long as they retain the confidence of the Lok Sabha.
- A confidence motion tests that support.

## Rule of Law and Fundamental Rights

- In the Keshavananda Bharati case (1973), the Supreme Court held that even Parliament cannot alter the Constitution’s basic structure.

## Secularism

- The S. R. Bommai case was decided in 1994.

## Fundamental Duties

Citizens should respect the Constitution, cherish the national flag, protect the environment, and promote harmony.

## Independent Judiciary

The Constitution provides for an independent judiciary.

## Universal Adult Franchise

- Every citizen aged 18 and above has the right to vote.

## Single Citizenship

India provides for single citizenship.

## Emergency Provisions

- The Centre can temporarily step in.
- **National Emergency** — a country-wide crisis threatening stability.
- **State Emergency** — a state government fails to function properly.
- **Financial Emergency** — the economy faces serious risks.

## Special Provisions for Certain States

These provisions allow states such as Nagaland, Mizoram, and Arunachal Pradesh to protect their cultural, social, and administrative uniqueness.

- Control over land and resources.
- Special autonomy in governance.
- Unique legislative powers.
- **Example:** The Inner Line Permit (ILP) system. Visitors from other states need special permission to enter certain areas of these states.

## Amendment Procedure

The Constitution is not rigid; it can be amended to adapt to changing times.

- Some amendments require a simple majority in Parliament.
- Amendments affecting the federal structure require ratification by at least half of the states.

## Fundamental Rights

- Right to equality.
- Right to freedom of speech and expression.
- Right against exploitation.
- Cultural and educational rights.
- Right to constitutional remedies.
- **Example:** The Kesavananda Bharati case (1973).

## Directive Principles of State Policy

These principles are guidelines for the government to create policies aimed at social justice, economic equality, and overall welfare.

- They are not legally enforceable.
- **Example:** The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA).

## Federalism

Federalism involves power-sharing between the Centre and the States.

- **Union List** — central government powers.
- **State List** — state government powers.
- **Concurrent List** — both levels can legislate.
- The Seventh Schedule lists subjects for the Union, State, and Concurrent Lists.
- **Example:** Defence is centrally controlled, while agriculture is state controlled.
