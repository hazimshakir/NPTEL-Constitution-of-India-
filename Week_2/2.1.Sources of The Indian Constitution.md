# Sources of the Indian Constitution

1. adopted the Constitution on 26th November 1949 and came into effect on 26th of January 1950.

## Sources

1. British Constitution for its parliamentary system
2. US Constitution for fundamental rights and judicial review
3. Irish Constitution for the Directive Principles of State Policy
4. Canadian Constitution for a strong federal center
5. Australian Constitution
6. Weimar Constitution of Germany
7. Soviet Union (USSR) Constitution.

## Government of India Act 1935

1. Longest act passed by the British government with 321 sections and 10 schedules.
2. Four sources:
   - a. Report of the Simon Commission
   - b. Third Round Table Conference
   - c. White Paper of 1933
   - d. Reports of the Joint select committees
3. Abolished the system of provincial dyarchy
4. Establishment of dyarchy at the centre and a ‘Federation of India’ consisting of the provinces of British India and most of the princely states.
5. Most importantly, the act established the office of the Governor
6. Features:
   - a. Federal scheme: Division of powers between the center and states.
   - b. Federal Legislature: The act suggested that the legislature will have two houses:
      - i. Council of States-Members :
         1. 156- British India
         2. 101 - Princely Indian states
      - ii. Federal Assembly- Members :
         1. 250 - British India
         2. 125 - Princely Indian states
7. Provincial Autonomy: Powers of the centre and provision were divided into three lists:
   - a. Federal list (59 items for the Centre)
   - b. Provincial list (54 items for Provinces)
   - c. Concurrent list (36 items for both)
   - d. The Residuary powers were handed over to the Viceroy
8. Office of the Governor
9. Public Service Commissions
10. Emergency provisions
11. Judiciary: The structure and powers of the judiciary

## British Constitution

1. Parliamentary form of government:availability of one or more opposition parties that exists to keep a check on the ruling party and its functioning.
2. Rule of Law: State is not governed by the representatives or the people of the country but governed by law.  (Article 14: Right to Equality)
3. Single citizenship: no dual citizenship
4. Writs: Supreme court and high courts have extraordinary powers to protect fundamental rights- Five Writs:
   - a. Habeas Corpus (produce the detained person before the court and release him if detention is found illegal)
   - b. Mandamus (an order from the Supreme Court or the High Court to a lower court to perform public duty)
   - c. Certiorari (SC or HC issues the writ for quashing the order already passed by an inferior court)
   - d. Prohibition (issued by the SC or the HC to a lower court to stop the latter from continuing with the procedures)
   - e. Quo-Warranto (restrains a person from holding a public office he is not entitled to hold)
5. Legislative procedure: The process of making laws.
6. Cabinet system: The structure and function of the cabinet.
7. Bicameralism: A two-house parliament.
8. Parliamentary privileges: Special rights and immunities of parliament members.

## US Constitution

1. Fundamental Rights: -Right to
   - a. Equality
   - b. Freedom
   - c. Against exploitation
   - d. Freedom of religion
   - e. Cultural and educational rights
   - f. Constitutional remedies
2. Judicial Review: Gives the judiciary an upper hand in interpreting the Constitution.
   - a. The Constitution has several basic features that cannot be amended.
   - b. first challenged by Justice JR Madholkar in the year 1964, in his dissent of the Sajjan Singh vs State of Rajasthan judgement.
   - c. The Kesavananda Bharati vs State of Kerala proceedings (1973)
   - d. Indira Gandhi violated the Doctrine with Emergency in 1975 and tried to prevent her prosecution by the 39th amendment
3. Impeachment of the President and Removal of Judges:
   - a. Judges of the supreme court can be removed by order of the president, addressed by each house of the parliament, and a majority greater than ⅔ on proved misbehaviour or incapacity.
4. Independence of the judiciary: No political interference on the judiciary.
5. Removal of Supreme Court and High Court judges
6. Vice-President

## Irish Constitution

1. Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP):
   - a. Socialist Directives
   - b. Gandhian Directives
   - c. Liberal Intellectual Directives.
2. Procedure for nomination of Rajya Sabha members
3. Method of election of the President

## Canadian Constitution

1. The provisions of a Federation with a strong centre
2. Residuary powers of the Centre
3. Appointment of State governors by the Centre
4. Advisory jurisdiction of the Supreme Court
