# Role and Structure of Indian Parliament

1. Article 79 - Parliament comprises the President of India, Lok Sabha (the House of the People) , and Rajya Sabha( the Council of States)
   - a. President of India
      - i. Summons Parliament sessions
      - ii. Can prorogue sessions
      - iii. Dissolve the Lok Sabha
      - iv. A bill becomes a law only after the President gives assent.
   - b. Lok Sabha - House of the People
      - i. lower house of Parliament
      - ii. members are elected through general elections
      - iii. has 543 elected members
      - iv. Each member represents a specific geographical constituency
      - v. Seats are allotted based on population - populational representation
      - vi. Uttar Pradesh has the highest number of seats - 80, Lakshadweep-1
      - vii. Normal term - five years.
      - viii. Lok Sabha is presided over by the Speaker - maintains order
      - ix. powers of the Lok Sabha
         1. Has special powers in financial matters
         2. Only the Lok Sabha can introduce Money Bills - Deal with taxation, expenditure, and government spending.
         3. Can pass a no-confidence motion against the Council of Ministers
   - c. Rajya Sabha- House of Elders
      - i. maximum of 245 members  - 233 elected indirectly by state + 12 nominated by the President for excellence in arts, literature, science, and social service - staggered six-year terms
      - ii. Every two years one-third of its members retire- Britain's House of Lords
      - iii. Can only recommend changes to Money Bills within 14 days
      - iv. Champions state interests for federal balance
   - d. Functions of Parliament
      - i. Legislation
         1. Makes laws on subjects mentioned in the Union List and the Concurrent List
         2. Bill is introduced-> debated in the House->examined by committees->sent to the other House->both Houses approve->President for assent
      - ii. financial control
         1. Taxes are imposed with parliamentary approval
         2. Money cannot be spent from the Consolidated fund of India without Parliament approval
         3. Every year, union budget->members debate allocations and priorities
      - iii. executive control
         1. Members ask questions during Question Hour
         2. Raise urgent matters during Zero Hour.
         3. No confidence motion
      - iv. Electoral responsibility
         1. Members of Parliament participate in the election of the President and Vice President.

- Longest parliamentary session ran 271 days in 1953
- shortest Lok  Sabha lasted just 18 months in 1970
