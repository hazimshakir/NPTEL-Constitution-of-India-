# Prime Minister

## Constitutional and Political Role

India’s first Prime Minister, Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru, once asked:

> “The future beckons to us. Whither do we go and what shall be our endeavour?”

That question continues to define the spirit of the office. The Prime Minister of India is not merely an administrator; he or she is the navigator who determines the direction in which the nation must move. Nehru’s vision of modern India laid the foundations of a parliamentary democracy rooted in progress, science, and freedom.

In India’s parliamentary system, the Prime Minister is the real executive authority: the head of the Council of Ministers, the chief policymaker, and the leader who connects the will of the people with the functioning of the state. The office connects the President and Parliament and ensures coherence in governance and coordination among ministries.

As constitutional expert M. V. Pylee remarked:

> “The Prime Minister is the keystone of the Cabinet arch. Remove him, and the whole structure collapses.”

Articles 74–78 of the Constitution define the structure, powers, and responsibilities of the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers, who together form the real executive authority of the Union.

- **Article 74(1):** Provides for a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at its head to aid and advise the President.
- **Article 74(2):** Protects the confidentiality of that advice by preventing courts from inquiring into it.
- **Article 75(1):** The Prime Minister is appointed by the President, while other ministers are appointed on the Prime Minister’s advice.

## Powers and Responsibilities

### Executive Role

The Prime Minister heads the Council of Ministers and exercises real executive authority.

- Recommends the appointment of ministers.
- Allocates and reshuffles portfolios.
- Can request ministers’ resignations.
- Advises the President on executive matters and important appointments, including Governors and Ambassadors.
- Chairs Cabinet meetings, settles disputes among ministers, and coordinates the work of ministries.
- Provides policy direction across defence, finance, education, and foreign affairs.
- Acts as the link between the President and the Council of Ministers.

### Legislative Role

The Prime Minister is the leader of the Lok Sabha and determines the government’s legislative agenda.

- Decides which bills and policies are introduced.
- Advises the President on summoning, proroguing, or dissolving the Lok Sabha.
- Plays a major role in parliamentary debates, policy statements, and law-making.
- Represents the government during Question Hour and confidence motions.

### Financial Role

- Plays a decisive role in preparing and presenting the Union Budget.
- Guides economic and development policies, including Five-Year Plans, reforms, and welfare schemes.
- Oversees financial management, economic strategy, and resource allocation across ministries.
- The economic philosophy of each Prime Minister has shaped the country’s direction, from Nehru’s planned economy and Indira Gandhi’s bank nationalisation to Manmohan Singh’s liberalisation and Narendra Modi’s Digital India and Make in India initiatives.

### Foreign Policy Role

- Represents India in international affairs, global summits, and diplomatic negotiations.
- Helps formulate and implement foreign policy and external relations.
- Engages with world leaders to promote India’s strategic, economic, and security interests.
- Foreign policy has reflected the visions of different Prime Ministers: Nehru’s Non-Alignment, Indira Gandhi’s assertive diplomacy, Vajpayee’s peace initiatives with neighbours, and Modi’s emphasis on connectivity, diaspora engagement, and global leadership.

Atal Bihari Vajpayee expressed the practical nature of diplomacy this way:

> “You can change friends, but not neighbours.”

### Political Role

- Leads the ruling party or coalition.
- Ensures unity within the party and coordinates among allies.
- Communicates government policy to the public.
- Leads the party in elections, campaigns, and public outreach.
- Balances relationships among the party, Parliament, and the people.

In coalition governments, the Prime Minister acts as a negotiator and consensus-builder, balancing multiple parties and ideologies to sustain stability. In majority governments, the office becomes the defining voice of governance and national direction.

### Role in Emergency Situations

- Exercises special powers during national emergencies and influences key decisions on security and governance.
- Guides the Cabinet on defence, internal security, and crisis management.

### Coordinating and Supervisory Role

- Ensures coordination among ministries and departments.
- Resolves inter-ministerial disputes.
- Maintains administrative efficiency.
- Reviews policy and programme implementation.

### Advisory Role to the President

- Advises the President on constitutional matters, including appointments and dissolutions.
- Acts of the President are carried out on the advice of the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers.

### Symbolic and Leadership Role

- Represents the unity and continuity of the government.
- Acts as the chief national leader and embodies the aspirations of the people.
- Provides moral leadership and inspires confidence during national and international challenges.

The office of the Prime Minister is therefore not merely administrative but transformative. It reflects the nation’s aspirations, challenges, and evolving identity. Leadership is about vision, responsibility, and wisdom. The Prime Minister embodies India in motion, while the President symbolises its constitutional soul. Together, they sustain the Republic—steady, strong, and free.

## Roles and Functions of the Prime Minister of India

### 1. Executive Role

- The Prime Minister is the head of the Council of Ministers and exercises real executive authority in the government.
- Appoints ministers, allocates and reshuffles their portfolios.
- Advises the President on all executive matters and on important appointments such as Governors, Ambassadors, and other key officials.
- Chairs Cabinet meetings, coordinates its work, and ensures unity of purpose and direction among ministries.
- Acts as a link between the President and the Council of Ministers, communicating decisions and advice.

### 2. Legislative Role

- Acts as the leader of the Lok Sabha (House of the People).
- Determines the legislative agenda of the government and introduces important bills and policies in Parliament.
- Advises the President on summoning, proroguing, or dissolving the Lok Sabha.
- Plays a major role in policy formulation, parliamentary debates, and law-making.
- Represents the government during question hours and motions of confidence in Parliament.

### 3. Financial Role

- Plays a decisive role in the preparation and presentation of the Union Budget.
- Guides the country’s economic and development policies, such as Five-Year Plans (earlier), reforms, and welfare schemes.
- Oversees financial management, economic strategy, and resource allocation across ministries.

### 4. Foreign Policy Role

- Represents India in international affairs, global summits, and diplomatic negotiations.
- Plays a key role in formulating and implementing India’s foreign policy and external relations.
- Engages with world leaders to promote India’s strategic, economic, and security interests abroad.

### 5. Political Role

- Serves as the leader of the ruling party or coalition, ensuring party unity and discipline.
- Acts as the chief spokesperson and political communicator of the government.
- Leads the party in elections, campaigns, and public outreach efforts.
- Balances relationships between the party, Parliament, and the people.

### 6. Role in Emergency Situations

- Exercises special powers during national emergencies, influencing key decisions on security and governance.
- Guides the Cabinet in defence, internal security, and crisis management.

### 7. Coordinating and Supervisory Role

- Ensures coordination among different ministries and departments.
- Resolves inter-ministerial disputes and maintains administrative efficiency.
- Reviews the progress of policies and programs, ensuring effective implementation.

### 8. Advisory Role to the President

- The Prime Minister advises the President on all constitutional matters, including appointments and dissolutions.
- Every act of the President is carried out on the advice of the Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers.

### 9. Symbolic and Leadership Role

- Represents the unity and continuity of the government.
- Acts as the chief national leader, embodying the aspirations of the people.
- Provides moral leadership and inspires confidence during national or international challenges.

### Role of the Prime Minister

### Powers and Functions

#### Executive Powers

The Prime Minister forms the Cabinet, allocates portfolios, coordinates its work, advises the President on key appointments from Governors to Ambassadors, and supervises the administration.

#### Legislative Powers

As leader of the Lok Sabha, the Prime Minister sets the legislative agenda, introduces major bills, decides when Parliament will meet, and advises the President on summoning and dissolving the Lok Sabha.

#### Financial Powers

Every Budget, Five-Year Plan, and major economic reform carries the Prime Minister’s imprint. From Nehru’s planned economy to Manmohan Singh’s liberalisation and Modi’s Digital India, the Prime Minister shapes the country’s economic vision.

#### Foreign Policy Powers

The Prime Minister represents India globally, negotiates treaties, leads international summits, and shapes strategic partnerships. Foreign policy often reflects the Prime Minister’s leadership style, from Indira Gandhi’s Non-Aligned stance to Vajpayee’s Lahore Bus Diplomacy and Modi’s Act East Policy.

#### Political Powers

As leader of the ruling party, the Prime Minister ensures unity and discipline and acts as the political communicator and bridge between the government and the people.

### Practical Role

In coalition governments, the Prime Minister acts as a consensus-builder, balancing multiple allies and ideologies. In majority governments, the Prime Minister often becomes the defining voice of governance.

From Nehru’s visionary modernism and Indira Gandhi’s centralisation of power to Modi’s emphasis on direct public engagement, each era has shaped the office anew.
